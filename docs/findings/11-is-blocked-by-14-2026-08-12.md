# #11 does not need a better search. It needs two segments removed.

Measured 2026-08-12 on `fan` frame 8, whose answer is known: position (3.3, −70.4, 22.1), focal
3043.

## The control fails, as the register said it would

`scripts/bootstrap_clip.py fan --frame 8` finds 12 cameras that fit the anchor and none is near the
truth. The best three:

```
worst probe   median   min samples   focal   position
        inf     12.8           364    4601   [119.6 -60.1  42.1]
        inf     11.4           181    1095   [-62.7  24.2  37.1]
        inf     11.4           181    1095   [ 62.7 -24.2  37.1]
nothing survived being carried — no seed found
```

The last two are the same camera under a half-turn, which is the measured degeneracy showing up
exactly where it should. `worst probe = inf` on every one: carried to the neighbouring frames they
score nothing at all.

**The gates are not what is wrong.** The obvious suspicion was that the `max_missing = 0.05`
plausibility gate rejects the true camera. It does not — the solved camera scores **0.0 % unmatched**
on frames 0, 8, 13, 19, 40 and 80. The truth would sail through.

## What is wrong is the input

Nine segments are detected on the anchor. Projecting the model through the true camera and asking
what each detected segment actually lies on:

| segment | family | length | what it is | distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | B | 169 px | box side | 3.4 px |
| **1** | A | 101 px | — | **55.0 px** |
| 2 | B | 501 px | box front near | 6.6 px |
| 3 | B | 355 px | box side | 2.4 px |
| 4 | A | 319 px | goal area far | 2.8 px |
| 5 | A | 908 px | goal line far | 4.7 px |
| **6** | A | 567 px | — | **60.0 px** |
| 7 | A | 673 px | box front far | 0.1 px |
| 8 | B | 151 px | goal area side | 1.7 px |

**Two of nine are not markings.** Segment 6 is 567 px long and 60 px from anything painted, so it is
not a short spurious fragment a length filter would catch — it is exactly the kind of long confident
non-marking #14 exists to reject.

Feeding the generator the same frame with and without those two:

| input | hypotheses | closest to the truth | focal |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 9 segments | 30 102 | **11.9 m** | 2205 — 28 % off |
| the 7 real ones | 15 842 | **3.7 m** | **3108 — 2.1 % off** |

Removing two segments out of nine moves the best camera available anywhere in the pool from 11.9 m
to 3.7 m, and the focal from 28 % wrong to 2 % wrong. Nothing about the search changed.

## Corrected the same day: frame 8 is not the clip

Everything above is one anchor frame. Sweeping four frames of `fan` and three of `broadcast`, and
reporting the closest hypothesis anywhere in the pool:

| clip / frame | all segments | inset > 30 | inset > 60 | inset > 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fan` 0 | **2.8 m** | 2.8 | 2.8 | 2.8 |
| `fan` 8 | 11.9 | **3.7** | 3.7 | 5.5 |
| `fan` 40 | **2.6 m** | 10.3 | 10.3 | 6.2 |
| `fan` 80 | 14.5 | 7.3 | 22.3 | 22.3 |
| `broadcast` 0 | **3.8 m** | 5.9 | 4.2 | 4.2 |
| `broadcast` 10 | 6.9 | 6.6 | 7.1 | 7.1 |
| `broadcast` 30 | 11.6 | 15.4 | 15.4 | 15.4 |

Two things follow, and both correct what is written above.

**The inset filter is refuted.** No threshold wins across the board: it helps on `fan` 8 and 80 and
*hurts* on `fan` 40 (2.6 → 10.3), `broadcast` 0 (3.8 → 5.9) and `broadcast` 30 (11.6 → 15.4). It
was designed on frame 8 and it works on frame 8. What it removes on the other frames is real
markings that genuinely run near the edge of the playing surface — the touchline above all, which
is the longest and most informative line in the picture.

**"#11 is not a ranking problem" was too strong.** On `fan` 0 and 40 the pool already contains a
hypothesis 2.6–2.8 m from the truth with **no filtering at all**. So on those frames nothing is
wrong with the input and the question is entirely whether the chooser finds it.

**#14's precision half has a measured payoff on the frames where the input is bad**, which is not
all of them. Yesterday's finding
(`14-has-nothing-left-to-buy-on-these-clips-2026-08-12.md`) said the precision half had no
measurable payoff, having looked only at the residual on solved clips. That was the wrong place to
look: on a solved clip the camera is already right and a spurious line changes nothing. The place a
non-marking costs is the **bootstrap**, where four correspondences are chosen out of nine and two of
the nine are wrong.

So the two tasks are one task, and the order is #14 precision first.

## What the two junk segments actually are

Drawn on the frame, both lie along the **join between the grass and the advertising boards** —
segment 6 runs 533 px down the foot of the LED hoarding on the far side, segment 1 is a short piece
of the same join further left. Not a shadow, not a mowing stripe, not a net: the boundary of the
playing surface itself.

That explains why every discriminator tried so far missed them. It is straight, because the
hoarding is straight. It is long — 567 px, longer than five of the seven real markings — so the
length filter that was "the only thing that helped" actively prefers it. And it is a genuine
brightness ridge with turf on one side.

It also suggests a test that is about position rather than appearance: a marking lies **inside** the
playing surface, the hoarding join lies **on its edge**. Measured as the median distance from a
segment to the outside of the surface mask, over whole clips:

| clip | real markings | non-markings |
|---|---|---|
| `fan` | 172 px median, p10 **93** | 20 px median, p90 **36** (n=34) |
| `broadcast` | 115 px median, p10 21 | 13 px (n=1) |
| `g15449383` | 81 px (n=1) | 80 px median, p90 94 (n=15) |

Separated cleanly on `fan` — p10 of the real markings is 93 px and p90 of the junk is 36 — and
**not established anywhere else**. `broadcast` yields one non-marking, which is the "seven samples
agreeing" trap in the register; `g15449383` shows no separation at all, though its "real" side is a
single segment. That clip is also the tight side-on shot where the surface is 22 % of the image, so
an absolute pixel threshold cannot serve all three.

Note also that the surface mask is morphologically closed with 45×45 then 61×61 kernels, so it
already extends up to ~30 px past the real turf edge. The junk's 20 px median on `fan` is that
inflation, not a real inset — which is why the threshold cannot be read off these numbers directly.

## The arc gate rejects the true camera on a zoomed frame

`bootstrap_clip.py` requires every candidate to put the centre circle or a penalty arc on real
paint: `an >= 8 and ad <= 6.0`. Its docstring argues that a camera putting them off-frame entirely
"has not scored badly, it has disqualified itself". Putting the **solved** camera through the same
gates:

| clip / frame | focal | median | miss | arc px | arc samples | the truth is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fan` 0 | 3046 | 0.97 | 0.0 % | 0.96 | 35 | kept |
| `fan` 8 | 3043 | 0.94 | 0.0 % | 1.02 | 35 | kept |
| **`fan` 40** | 4499 | 0.73 | 0.0 % | — | **0** | **REJECTED** |
| **`fan` 80** | 4781 | 0.78 | 0.0 % | — | **0** | **REJECTED** |
| `broadcast` 0 / 10 / 30 | ~4370 | ≤0.94 | ≤2.5 % | ≤2.49 | 35 | kept |

On `fan` 40 and 80 the operator has zoomed to 4499 and 4781 px and **no arc is in the picture at
all**. The gate cannot distinguish "this camera hides the arcs" from "this frame has no arcs to
show", and treats the second as the first. That is the R-6 rule — mark, never hide — broken against
the solver instead of against a player: a subject that cannot be measured is being scored as wrong.

It is also the direct cause of `bootstrap_clip.py fan --frame 40` printing *"no plausible camera at
all on the anchor frame"* on a frame whose hypothesis pool contains a camera **2.6 m from the
truth**.

## What is not established

The hypothesis count moves with `--max-hypotheses` in a way this probe did not explain: the cap at
60 000 produced 7 430 hypotheses and the cap at 200 000 produced 30 102, on the same nine segments.
So the register's "4 000, 20 000 and 60 000 return the identical wrong camera" is not the
exhaustion it reads like, and whatever it is has not been measured here. Do not repeat that line as
if it were understood.

The 3.7 m figure is the best hypothesis *available*, not the one the ranking would pick. Whether
the existing chooser finds it once the junk is gone is the next measurement, not a result of this
one.

Measured on one frame of one clip. The junk fraction on other clips and other frames is unknown.

Put the SOLVED camera through the bootstrap's gates and `fan` 40 and 80 are thrown out by the arc
test — the operator has zoomed to 4499 and 4781 px, no arc is in the picture, `arc_n = 0` reads as

| anchor | strict | abstaining |

| `fan` 0 | 1.0 px, 3.11 m | 1.2 px, 3.26 m — diluted |

## Parallax cannot separate a marking from the hoarding join

The remaining geometric idea for #14's precision half: a marking lies on the grass plane and the
advertising-board join is raised above it, so under a plane-to-plane homography between frames the
first maps exactly and the second carries a parallax error.

It needs the camera to MOVE. Measured on all three solved clips:

| clip | step between frames, median | max | spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fan` | 0.0000 m | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| `broadcast` | 0.0000 m | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| `CRO_MOR_194948` | 0.0000 m | 0.000 | 0.000 |

Zero, exactly — `solve_shared_centre` collapses the camera to one point and the paint prefers that,
so these are pure rotations. A pure rotation produces no parallax at any height, so the test has
nothing to measure. Refuted for every clip on disk; it becomes possible only on a clip that really
travels, which is #23 and does not exist yet.

# The proximity measure was wrong, and so were the numbers built on it

Everything above that says "the pool holds a hypothesis N metres from the truth" measured the
distance between **optical centres**. That is not a distance between cameras.

On `broadcast` frame 0 the hypothesis whose centre sits 3.83 m from the truth has a focal of
**20000** — pinned at the search bound, +355 % — and matches **zero** lines, so its residual is not
even finite. Refitting it moves it nowhere, because with no correspondences the objective is a
constant. A camera is position, orientation and focal at once, and only its projection tests all
three.

`bench_bootstrap_gates.py` now measures the **reprojection gap**: the median pixel distance between
where the truth puts the pitch and where the candidate does, over the samples the truth has inside
the frame. Numbers quoted before this — 11.9 m, 3.7 m, 2.6 m, 3.8 m — are position-only and should
not be reused.

## The junk-segment story holds for `fan` and not for `broadcast`

Measured the same way as `fan` frame 8, projecting the truth and asking what each detected segment
lies on:

| clip / frame | segments | not on any marking |
|---|---|---|
| `fan` 8 | 9 | **2** |
| `fan` 0 | 7 | 0 |
| `broadcast` 0 | 9 | **0** |
| `broadcast` 10 | 7 | **0** |

So `broadcast` fails with a clean input. Whatever blocks it is not #14, and "the two tasks are one
task" — written above on the strength of one frame of one clip — is too broad. It holds where junk
exists.

different question from #14.


## Why `broadcast` fails: the second family has nothing in it

With the reprojection gap as the measure, the pool's best candidate on `broadcast` puts the pitch
**248.5 px** (frame 0) and **374.3 px** (frame 10) from where the truth does, focal 68–71 % wrong.
Against `fan` frame 0, where it is **1.2 px** and kept. So the generator never proposes the answer
on `broadcast`, and the gates, the junk and the refit are all downstream of that.

The generator enumerates two lines from each vanishing-point family. Splitting the detections and
asking what each lies on:

| | family A | family B |
|---|---|---|
| `fan` 0 | box front near, box side, goal-area side, box side | **goal area far, box front far, goal line** |
| `broadcast` 0 | box side, goal line, goal-area side, touchline, box side | **the near penalty ARC ×3, goal-area side** |

`fan`'s second family holds three distinct straight markings at three known positions. `broadcast`'s
holds one short side line and **three separate detections of the same curved marking** — marking 22,
the near penalty arc, 16.9 m of path across a 14.6 m chord. Hough fits straight segments to a curve
and returns several, none of which is a straight marking and all of which are the same object.

So `broadcast` has no two independent markings in its second family, and a 2+2 enumeration cannot
produce the right camera from it. That is not #14 — every detection is on real paint — and not a
threshold. It is the detector returning chords of an arc as if they were lines, on a clip whose
geometry leaves the cross-family markings short and few.

`measure/ellipse.py` already models the arcs for scoring. Nothing feeds them to the bootstrap.


## Feeding the arcs to the generator: the prerequisite fails

The obvious next move after the above — `broadcast` has one usable straight marking in its second
family and an arc, so give the arc to the generator. `measure/ellipse.py` already fits one.

It does not find the arcs on these frames. Measured against the truth's own predicted conic:

| clip / frame | axis ratio | inliers | fit rms | distance to the nearest real arc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `broadcast` 0 | 10.7 | 868 | 0.96 px | **96 px** |
| `broadcast` 10 | 7.0 | 728 | 1.23 px | **122 px** |
| `fan` 0 | 2.6 | 480 | 1.24 px | **57 px** |
| `fan` 40 | 9.8 | 345 | 1.24 px | **140 px** |
| `CRO_MOR_194948` 0 | 10.9 | 325 | 1.69 px | **129 px** |

Axis ratios of 7–11 with a major axis up to 4312 px are the failure `ellipse.py`'s own comment
names: "the fit finds the straightest thing available… lines with a conic drawn through them". The
`MAX_AXIS_RATIO = 12.0` guard does not catch them, and tightening it to 5 changes the answer on
`broadcast` 0 without bringing it any closer to the arc — 180 px either way at the time, 96 px once
the measure was repaired.

So the arc is not available to give to anything, and building the generator extension on it would
have been building on a fit that is 57–140 px from the marking it claims.

**The measure had to be repaired first.** `conic_disagreement(a, b, pts)` never read `a` — its body
was `_distance(b, pts)`. Four different fitted ellipses all "disagreed" with the same predicted arc
by exactly 180.2 px, which is what gave it away. Fixed, and validated against circles 20 px apart
before being believed.
